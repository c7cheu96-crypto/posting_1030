Star Chef Choi Hyun-seok's Jungang Gamsokgi in Seongsu, Seoul: Mala Cream Jjamppong Worth the Trip

If you follow Korean food TV, you may know Chef Choi Hyun-seok, one of the country's best-known star chefs, seen on shows like Culinary Class Wars (흑백요리사) and Please Take Care of My Refrigerator (냉장고를 부탁해). His Chinese-Korean restaurant Jungang Gamsokgi (중앙감속기), which also goes by the English name Central Reducer, sits in Seongsu-dong, Seoul's trendy neighborhood of cafés and converted warehouses. I booked a table in advance and finally went. 🌶️

First impressions: a line outside, and space inside

The building is wrapped in weathered wooden planks with the name in big letters, and when I arrived there were already guests waiting on the bench out front. Good news: I had a reservation through CatchTable, Korea's most popular restaurant booking app, which I'd strongly recommend here.

One practical note: there's no private parking, as the sign by the door politely points out, so the subway or a nearby public lot is the way to go.

Inside, the ceilings are high and the open kitchen runs along the back. My favorite part was how much space there is between tables. You can actually relax and talk without bumping elbows, which is rare at a popular Seoul spot.

You order on a tablet at your table, and it has an English menu option, which is a big plus for visitors. We chose the Set A for two (57,400 won): basil shrimp spring rolls, balsamic guobaorou, brisket mala cream jjamppong, and two soft drinks.

Basil shrimp spring rolls

A crispy start. The wrapper shatters nicely, and inside there's shrimp with a fresh hint of basil. The tangy red dipping sauce really wakes up your appetite.

Brisket mala cream jjamppong: the must-order

Jjamppong (짬뽕) is the classic spicy seafood noodle soup of Korean-Chinese restaurants. This version mixes in mala, the tingly, numbing Sichuan spice, and a creamy broth. The result is silky and rich at first, then the mala tingle slowly builds. With beef brisket, seafood and bok choy, it was honestly the best thing we ate. I'd come back just for this bowl.

Balsamic guobaorou

Guobaorou (꿔바로우) is a crispy fried pork dish from northeastern China, very popular in Korea. Here it gets a creative twist: a dark balsamic glaze and a snowfall of grated cheese. Sweet, tangy and savory all at once, it's a playful, chef-driven take on a familiar favorite.

Good to know

💡 Book ahead on CatchTable; walk-ins may face a wait.
💡 No private parking, so take public transport.
💡 The ordering tablet supports English.
💡 Set A for two is 57,400 won and covers three dishes plus two drinks.

The verdict

It isn't cheap, but honestly, once you taste the food it feels like great value for the quality. Spacious seating, creative dishes from a celebrity chef, and that unforgettable mala cream jjamppong make Jungang Gamsokgi an easy recommendation if you're exploring Seongsu. Happy eating! 🧡
